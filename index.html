Interactive Theoretical Principles, Vector Formulas & Scoring Specification

1. Ray Parametrization & Chamber Geometry

A laser ray originates from emitter origin point O and propagates along normalized unit direction vector D:

Ray Vector Equation P(t) = O + t · D, for t ≥ 0

Chamber walls, optical mirrors, and detector plates are modeled as 2D planar line segments bounded between endpoints A and B:

Line Segment Equation Q(s) = A + s · (B − A), for s ∈ [0, 1]

2. Nearest Positive Intersection

Equating P(t) = Q(s) yields a system of two linear equations solved using 2D cross-product determinants:

Distance & Segment Scalars t = ((A − O) × (B − A)) / (D × (B − A)) s = ((A − O) × D) / (D × (B − A))

A valid optical collision occurs when the determinant denominator ≠ 0, segment scalar s ∈ [0, 1], and ray distance t > ε (where ε = 0.5 px). The raycaster checks all boundaries to find the minimal positive distance t_min, halting the beam at the nearest occluding surface.

Step 1: Calibration Mode

In initial Step 1 calibration mode, mirrors act as solid non-reflective barriers (pure ray-occlusion mode). The laser stops immediately upon contact with the mirror surface, visually proving accurate collision point calculation, parametric clipping, and nearest obstacle sorting.

1. Vector Law of Specular Reflection

When the laser impacts an optical mirror at point P_hit, the reflected direction vector R is computed using Snell's specular reflection law:

Specular Reflection Vector Formula R = D − 2 · (D · N) · N

2. Multi-Bounce & Epsilon Origin Offset

To prevent floating-point rounding errors from causing the ray to immediately re-collide with the exact same mirror at t ≈ 0, the reflection origin is shifted forward along R:

Secondary Ray Origin O_next = P_hit + R · ε, where ε = 0.5 px

The raycaster recurses up to 8 bounces. Propagation terminates if the beam strikes a wall, reaches receiver sensor aperture, or exhausts maximum bounces.

Surface Normal & Snell's Law

Incident angle θᵢ and reflected angle θᵣ are evaluated relative to surface normal N:

Equal Angles Theorem θᵢ = arccos(−D · N) ≡ θᵣ = arccos(R · N)

Both angles are strictly identical according to Snell's law and are continuously measured and displayed in degrees in the live telemetry panel.

1. Kinematic Positioning & Clamping

Optical mirrors support direct pointer and touch dragging. Coordinates are mapped from screen space to tactical chamber space and clamped to the perimeter chamber walls with safe margins so optical elements cannot escape the containment volume.

2. Rotational Actuator Synchronization

Mirror geometry is defined by center position (x, y), span length L, and rotation angle θ. Both endpoints are recalculated dynamically in real time:

Segment Endpoints Transformation A = (x − (L/2)·cos θ, y − (L/2)·sin θ) B = (x + (L/2)·cos θ, y + (L/2)·sin θ)

This guarantees 100% visual and mathematical alignment between rendered graphics and ray collision segments.

3. Discrete Move Registration

To ensure fair gameplay scoring, continuous dragging does not flood the move counter. A move is only committed when the pointer is released and displacement exceeds threshold:

Commit Threshold Δd > 3 px OR Δθ > 0.8°

Small accidental touches are disregarded, protecting your efficiency score.

1. Real-Time Telemetry & Scoring Model

The telemetry subsystem samples the laser beam at 60 FPS, calculating reflection counts, incidence angles, impact targets, and receiver alignment.

Chamber Score Evaluation Formula Score = max(0, 1000 − 50·M − 100·B_extra + T_bonus)
  • M: Number of committed orientation adjustments.
  • B_extra: Surplus bounces beyond the optimal sector path.
  • T_bonus: Time efficiency bonus granted upon victory.
